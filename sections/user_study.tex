\section{User study}\label{user_study}
A user study is an approach taken to understand the requirements of users in relation to your application \parencite{user_study_definition}. It can be used to understand the user requirements better for those that will be using the system and consequently improve design decisions. 

\subsection{Study design}
The user study was designed with the goal of being able to evaluate the effectiveness of the tool, \lintex, and identifying areas of improvement for the tool. To meet this goal, the user study was split into six key stages:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item An introductionary stage, with its goal being to identify where the participant's knowledge is around in related topics.\label{user_study:intro_questions}
	\item An initial listening of the inaccessible document produced for this user study.\label{user_study:initial_listening}
	\item A questioning stage based on the listening of the inaccessible document.\label{user_study:initial_questions}
	\item A think-aloud session for understanding the accessibility of LinTeX.\label{user_study:using_lintex}
	\item A final listening of the newly created accessible document.\label{user_study:final_listening}
	\item A questioning stage based on the listening of the accessible document.\label{user_study:final_questions}
\end{enumerate}
These stages allowed for a comparison of the before and after of using LinTeX, through stages \ref{user_study:initial_listening}  and \ref{user_study:final_listening}, as well as associated questioning stages \ref{user_study:initial_questions} and \ref{user_study:final_questions}, of which allow both qualitative and quantitative data to be received.
